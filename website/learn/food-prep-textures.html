Safe serving sizes and textures by age

How to shape and soften foods for 6 to 8 months and 9 to 12 months, and the squish test to check softness.

3 min read ·

In this article
  1. Changing textures as your baby grows
  2. The squish test

In short

  • 6 to 8 months: soft, finger-length strips your baby can hold in a fist.
  • 9 to 12 months: small soft pieces about the size of a chickpea.
  • Many parents use the squish test: it squashes easily between your thumb and finger.

Changing textures as your baby grows

Preparing food safely means adjusting how soft it is and its shape as your baby’s skills grow.

  • 6 to 8 months, finger-length strips: long, thick shapes about 3 to 4 inches long that your baby can hold in a fist. Soft enough to squash easily between your thumb and finger.
  • 9 to 12 months, small soft pieces: small pieces about the size of a chickpea as the pincer grasp develops. Soft and tender enough to mash with the gums.

The squish test

Before offering a solid food, press it firmly between your thumb and index finger. If it squashes easily without much force, it’s soft enough for your baby to mash with their gums.

Safety and allergen reminder

Serve foods in shapes, sizes and textures that suit your baby's age and chewing skills, such as thin slices, mashed or pureed, rather than whole round pieces. When offering a common allergen, watch your baby closely for signs of a reaction, and ask your pediatrician about timing and how often to offer it.
