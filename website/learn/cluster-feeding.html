Decoding cluster feeding

Why some babies want to feed every 30 to 60 minutes for hours, especially in the evening, and how to get through it.

4 min read

In short

  • Frequent evening feeds are usually a normal phase, often during growth spurts.
  • Plenty of wet and dirty diapers and good growth are reassuring signs.
  • Set up a feeding station, and share the soothing with a partner.

The biology behind the fussy hours

Cluster feeding is when a baby wants to nurse or take a bottle every 30 to 60 minutes over several hours. It often happens in the late afternoon or evening. It can leave parents worried, but it’s usually a normal phase.

  • Making milk: frequent nursing signals your body to make more milk to keep up with your growing baby.
  • Growth spurts: cluster feeding often lines up with growth spurts, commonly around 3 weeks, 6 weeks, 3 months and 6 months.
  • Day and night rhythms: evening breast milk contains more melatonin, which may help your baby develop day and night rhythms.

Survival strategies

  • Reframe it: cluster feeding is your baby communicating a need. It usually isn’t a sign that you don’t have enough milk, as long as your baby has plenty of wet and dirty diapers and is growing well. If you’re worried, talk to your pediatrician or a breastfeeding specialist.
  • Set up a station: have water, high-protein snacks and something to read or watch ready before the usual evening cluster begins.
  • Divide and conquer: a partner can carry, bathe or soothe the baby between feeds to give the feeding parent short breaks.
