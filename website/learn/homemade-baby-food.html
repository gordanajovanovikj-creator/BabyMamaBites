Making and storing baby food

A few hours of prep can stock your freezer for weeks.

3 min read

In this article
  1. Make it
  2. Store it

In short

  • Cook until soft, then puree or mash with no added salt or seasoning.
  • Homemade food spoils faster: refrigerate leftovers and check before serving.
  • Freeze purees in ice-cube trays and use within 3 months.

Make it

Pick some fruits, vegetables and grains. Cook them until soft enough to mash easily with a fork; ripe banana and avocado can be mashed raw. Puree or mash, and thin with breast milk, formula or water.

  • Cook with no added salt or seasoning.
  • Remove fat, skin and bones from meat, poultry and fish before cooking.
  • Remove seeds and hard pits from fruit.

Store it

Homemade food isn't bacteria-free and spoils faster than food from a jar. Refrigerate what you don't use and check for signs of spoilage before serving.

To freeze, spoon purees into ice-cube trays. Once frozen, move the cubes to labeled, dated bags and use them within 3 months. Later, mix cubes to make new combinations.
