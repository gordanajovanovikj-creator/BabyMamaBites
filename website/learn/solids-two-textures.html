One meal, two textures: how to feed yourself and your baby from the same pot

Cook one base meal, set aside your baby’s portion before seasoning, and change the texture so you both eat the same food.

3 min read

In short

  • Cook one base meal, and scoop out your baby's portion before adding salt or spice.
  • Change the texture to suit your baby: mashed, soft strips or small pieces.
  • Introduce allergens on their own before mixing them into family meals.

One pot, two eaters

Feeding a baby who is moving on to solid foods is an exciting milestone, but making separate meals for adults and babies can quickly lead to kitchen burnout.

The secret to simpler mealtimes, without giving up flavor for adults or safety for little ones, is “one meal, two textures.” Take a single base meal, remove your baby’s portion before you add high-sodium or spicy ingredients, and change its texture. That way you can feed yourself and your baby with the exact same ingredients.

Why “one meal, two textures” works

  • Saves time and energy: you only cook once, with less prep and fewer dishes.
  • Encourages healthy eating: babies learn by watching. When they see you eating the same colorful, nutrient-dense foods, they are more likely to accept new flavors.
  • Builds eating skills: changing textures gradually lets your baby develop chewing skills safely as they grow.

Golden rules of family cooking with a baby

  • Hold the salt and sugar: babies’ kidneys can’t handle much sodium, and the USDA Dietary Guidelines advise no added sugars before age 2. Cook the base meal without added salt or sugar; adults can season their own plates at the table.
  • Watch common allergens: introduce top allergens (like peanut butter, eggs, dairy and sesame) one at a time first, before mixing them into family meals.
  • Mind choking hazards: whole nuts, whole grapes, raw hard vegetables and large chunks of meat are choking risks. Make sure your baby’s portion is soft, mashable, or cut into age-appropriate strips.
