From messy to skilled: teaching your baby to use a spoon

Spoon skills build slowly. A three-phase approach from pre-loaded spoons at 6 months to independent scooping after 12 months.

3 min read

In short

  • Spoon skills grow in phases, so don't expect neat scooping early.
  • Start with pre-loaded spoons, then try two spoons, then independent scooping.
  • Messy hands-on play builds comfort with new textures.

How spoon skills develop

Using a spoon takes planning, a steady shoulder, wrist turning and depth perception. Expecting an 8-month-old to scoop and bring food to their mouth without spilling is asking too much. Spoon skills grow in phases.

Three phases

  • Phase 1 (6 to 8 months), pre-loaded spoons: load puree or mash onto a textured dipping spoon and hand it to your baby to bring to their own mouth.
  • Phase 2 (9 to 11 months), the two-spoon strategy: your baby holds one spoon to explore while you use a second spoon to feed or show scooping.
  • Phase 3 (12 months and up), independent scooping: your baby dips into the bowl and turns their wrist toward their mouth.

The value of sensory play

Letting your baby touch, squish and smear food builds confidence and comfort with food. Exploring with their hands helps your baby learn where their mouth is, and getting used to textures on their skin makes new textures in the mouth less surprising.

Every child is different

Every child's relationship with food develops in its own way. These are supportive ideas, not clinical solutions. If your child strongly refuses food, is losing weight or has trouble swallowing, talk to your pediatrician or a pediatric feeding specialist.
